Actual Budget bank sync in Europe: every option in 2026

Actual Budget doesn't need a bank connection to work, but typing in every coffee gets old fast. If your bank is in Europe, there are four ways to get transactions in today: two free, one closed to newcomers, and one paid. This guide covers what each needs and where each falls short.

I'm David Alves, and I make Bridge Bank, one of the options here. The free ones come first, because for plenty of people they're enough.

The short answer

  • You don't mind a few minutes a week: download a file from your bank and import it into Actual. Free, and nothing to set up.
  • You want it automatic and free, and you're fine with an experimental feature: turn on Actual's own Enable Banking sync. It needs Actual on an HTTPS address, and it syncs only while Actual is open.
  • You want it to run on its own, even with Actual closed: Bridge Bank syncs on your server on a schedule, and emails you when something needs you.
  • GoCardless, the old default, isn't taking new accounts.

Free: import your bank's file

Almost every European bank lets you download your transactions from its website or app. Actual reads CSV, OFX, QFX and QIF files, plus CAMT.053 (an XML format many European banks offer).

  1. Download a file from your bank

    Look for "export", "download" or "statement" in your online banking, and pick any of the formats above.

  2. Import it into the matching account

    In Actual, open the account and click Import. For a CSV, tell Actual which column is which once; it remembers for next time. Transactions you already have are matched, so importing overlapping dates doesn't create doubles.

Good for: anyone who reconciles once a week anyway. Falls short: it's manual, and your budget is only as fresh as your last download.

Free: Actual's own Enable Banking sync

Since 2026, Actual has its own connection to Enable Banking, a regulated open banking provider that covers banks across Europe. It's marked experimental: Actual's settings warn that experimental features "may cause irrecoverable data loss", and its guide suggests the nightly version for the newest fixes.

  1. Switch it on

    In Actual's Settings, open Experimental features (among the advanced settings) and turn on Enable Banking sync (EU banks). It only appears when you use an Actual server.

  2. Register an application at Enable Banking

    Create a free account at Enable Banking, register a new application in Production mode, and set its redirect URL to your own Actual address followed by /enablebanking/auth_callback. That address has to start with https. Then link your own bank accounts to the application in Enable Banking's control panel; without that, Actual shows no banks.

  3. Connect it in Actual

    Under More, Bank Sync, choose Set up Enable Banking, paste the application ID and upload the key file. Then in an account, choose Link account, Enable Banking, your country and your bank.

Good for: people who already reach Actual over HTTPS (a domain with a certificate, or a tunnel) and open it most days. Falls short: automatic sync is off by default, and when you turn it on, it runs only while Actual is open in a browser somewhere. Close it for a week and nothing arrives until you open it again. And it's experimental, so it can change between versions.

What happened to GoCardless

For years, Actual's European bank sync went through GoCardless Bank Account Data (formerly Nordigen). Actual's bank sync page now says GoCardless is not accepting new accounts. If you already have one, it keeps working in Actual; if you don't, it's no longer an option. Actual's other providers are for other regions: SimpleFIN for North America, Pluggy.ai for Brazil and Akahu for New Zealand.

Automatic on your server: Bridge Bank

Bridge Bank is a small Docker container that runs next to Actual and syncs your bank on a schedule you pick, every 6, 12 or 24 hours, whether or not Actual is open. It uses Enable Banking too, read-only, for 2,500+ banks in 29 countries.

What it does differently from Actual's built-in sync:

  • It runs on its own. The schedule lives on your server, not in a browser tab.
  • No HTTPS address of your own needed. Your bank sends you back through a page on bridgebank.app, which hands you over to your own Bridge Bank. Actual can stay on your home network.
  • It tells you when something's wrong. An email when a sync fails, and before a bank connection expires (banks ask you to renew every 90 to 180 days).
  • Transfers link themselves. A transfer between two of your own accounts is matched on your account numbers, even when every account has the same name.
  • Pending transactions are updated when they settle, and duplicates are skipped.

  1. Register an Enable Banking application

    Same free account as above, with https://bridgebank.app/callback as the redirect URL. The setup guide has every field to copy.

  2. Start Bridge Bank

    One command starts it next to Actual. A short wizard asks for your license key and where Actual lives.

  3. Connect your bank

    Pick your bank and approve read-only access on its own login page. Each bank account fills a plain Actual account; don't also link that account inside Actual, or every transaction arrives twice.

The trial is free for 7 days and needs no card. A license is €29, once, with updates included and two bank accounts; extra accounts are €10 each. It works with plain Actual and with Bridge Budget.

Questions

Is Enable Banking free?
Registering an application and linking your own accounts doesn't cost anything. Both Actual's built-in sync and Bridge Bank use that free route.
Can Enable Banking or Bridge Bank move my money?
No. The access you approve on your bank's page is read-only: balances and transactions. Neither ever sees your bank password.
How often do I have to log in to my bank again?
Banks ask you to renew open banking access every 90 to 180 days, whichever way you connect. Bridge Bank emails you before it runs out; renewing takes your bank login and a click.
Can I use Actual's sync for one bank and Bridge Bank for another?
Yes, as long as each Actual account has only one source. An account linked both ways gets every transaction twice.
My bank isn't in Europe. What then?
Look at Actual's bank sync page for SimpleFIN (North America), Pluggy.ai (Brazil) or Akahu (New Zealand), or import your bank's file.
